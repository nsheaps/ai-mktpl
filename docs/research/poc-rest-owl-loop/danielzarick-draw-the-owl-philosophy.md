# Draw the Owl — Daniel Zarick

**URL**: https://www.danielzarick.com/blog/draw-the-owl/
**Date read**: 2026-03-20

## Key Takeaways

- The "draw the owl" philosophy is about **self-directed problem solving** — start with what you can figure out yourself, then ask targeted questions when stuck
- Nobody can lay out all the steps for you because nobody else has been in your exact situation
- Progress comes from **taking action first**, not waiting for a complete roadmap
- Criticizes broad questions ("How do I build X?") in favor of discrete challenges ("What's the right data model for this entity?")
- The owl emerges through your own effort and targeted feedback

## Relevance to poc-rest-owl-loop Plugin

This is actually the **opposite** of our plugin's philosophy. Zarick argues you should struggle through it yourself. Our plugin argues the AI _can_ lay out all the steps — because it has access to the entire internet's worth of knowledge about what "all the steps" look like.

The synthesis: the plugin handles the _known unknowns_ (competitive research, feature matrices, standard patterns) while the user handles the _unknown unknowns_ (their specific differentiation, target audience, taste). That's why Phase 0 (intake) and user checkpoints between phases are critical — they're the "targeted questions when stuck" that Zarick advocates.

## Criticism

The blog post romanticizes the struggle. For software projects, much of the "owl" is boilerplate that has been drawn thousands of times before. Research, specs, and testing infrastructure are largely templatable.
